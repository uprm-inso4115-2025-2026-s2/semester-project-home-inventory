== Scenarios

Scenarios describe realistic end-to-end situations in the domain and how stakeholders expect things to work.

=== Scenario S1a — Low-Stock Trigger After Partial Consumption
* Persona/actor: Julian, the health-conscious student; household member.
* Context: Julian keeps a personal shelf of meal-prep items separate from roommates. He depends on Barilla gluten-free elbows, which require online ordering with 3-day shipping; his low-stock threshold for this item is 2 boxes.
* Trigger: Julian records consumption after Sunday meal prep, reducing tracked quantity from 3 boxes to 1 — below his threshold of 2.
* Expected state transition: NORMAL → LOW_STOCK

.Steps
. Julian opens the system after meal prep and selects "Barilla gluten-free elbows" from his personal shelf.
. He used 2 boxes for the week's meal prep and decreases the count from 3 to 1.
. The system compares the new count to the threshold and flags the item as low-stock.
. The system adds it to the restock list/planner with status "Needs restock" and reason "LOW_STOCK".
. The system presents a low-stock alert: "Barilla gluten-free elbows is low — 1 box remaining."

.Expected outcome
* Inventory shows "Barilla gluten-free elbows: 1 box" with a LOW_STOCK flag.
* The item appears in the restock list/planner as needing replenishment.
* Julian has enough lead time to reorder before running out mid-week.

=== Scenario S1b — Low-Stock and Out-of-Stock Simultaneously
* Persona/actor: Julian, the health-conscious student; household member.
* Context: Same as S1a, but Julian had only 2 boxes remaining before dinner; his threshold is 2 boxes.
* Trigger: Julian uses both remaining boxes, decreasing the count from 2 to 0.
* Expected state transition: NORMAL → OUT_OF_STOCK

.Steps
. Julian opens the system after meal prep and selects "Barilla gluten-free elbows."
. He used both remaining boxes and decreases the count to 0.
. The system detects the new count is both below threshold and at zero, and flags the item OUT_OF_STOCK.
. The system creates a single combined alert: "Barilla gluten-free elbows is out of stock." Out-of-stock takes priority over low-stock to avoid redundant alerts.
. The restock list/planner entry is created with reason "OUT_OF_STOCK."

.Expected outcome
* Julian receives one alert, not two.
* The restock list/planner shows the item with the most urgent reason.
* Julian's entire meal plan for the week is at risk; the alert conveys urgency.

.Related needs / stories
* Needs: N1, N2
* User stories: US-ALT-01, US-INV-01

=== Scenario S2 — Shared Inventory Prevents Duplicate Purchase
* Persona/actor: Sofia, the budget-conscious roommate; Andrea, the unofficial household manager; household members.
* Context: Sofia and her roommates share essentials. Past issues include duplicate purchases from out-of-date whiteboards and unclear responsibility. Sofia has a strict $50 weekly grocery budget.
* Trigger: Sofia is at the supermarket about to buy Charmin Ultra Soft toilet paper and checks the shared inventory before paying.
* Expected state transition: No state change for the item; shared visibility converges after synchronization.

.Steps
. Sofia opens the shared inventory at the supermarket and searches "toilet paper."
. The system displays: "Charmin Ultra Soft toilet paper — In stock: 24 rolls."
. Sofia sees it is sufficiently stocked, returns the 12-pack, and saves $8.99 of her budget.
. Later, Andrea records a purchase of Dawn dish soap; the shared inventory updates for all members once recorded and synchronized.

.Expected outcome
* Sofia avoids a duplicate purchase, keeping $8.99 within her budget.
* All household members see the updated shared inventory after synchronization.

.Related needs / stories
* Needs: N3, N4, N5
* User stories: US-INV-02, US-SB-01, US-RPT-01
