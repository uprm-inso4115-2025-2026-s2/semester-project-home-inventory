= Stakeholder Persona Sharing & Budget

== Overview

This document shows a stakeholder persona for the Sharing & Budget team. The persona is a fictional but realistic
person that captures the development team's understanding of a key
stakeholder group, in this case, the household member who actively
participates in shared budget tracking.

== Persona: Sofia Marrero

=== Personal Background

* *Name:* Sofia Marrero
* *Age:* 27
* *Place of residence:* San Juan, Puerto Rico
* *Interests:* Cooking at home, weekend hiking, tracking personal
  finances, splitting costs fairly with roommates
* *Living situation:* Shares an apartment with two other people, a
  close friend and an acquaintance referred by that friend

Sofia is organized by habit. She keeps a notes app with her monthly
expenses and checks her bank account every few days. She is not an
accountant, but she cares about knowing where her money goes and
whether shared costs are being handled fairly.

---

=== Professional Background

* *Employment:* Graphic designer at a small marketing agency
* *Position:* Individual contributor, no direct reports
* *Decision-making responsibility:* Low at work, but high within her
  household, she is the one who usually brings up conversations about
  splitting rent, groceries, and utilities
* *Degree of influence:* Informal household leader on financial matters,
  the others tend to defer to her when there is a dispute about costs

---

=== Stakeholder Group

Sofia belongs to the *household member* stakeholder group. She joined
the household in the app after accepting an invitation from her friend,
who created the household. She did not set up the budget but she
interacts with it regularly, logging expenses, checking the remaining
balance, and reviewing what others have spent.

---

=== Key Usage Goals

* Know at a glance how much of the shared budget has been used and
  how much remains.
* Log a shared expense quickly after it happens, without navigating
  through multiple screens.
* See how an expense was split, who owes what, without having to
  ask her roommates directly.
* Trust that the numbers in the app are accurate and up to date.

---

=== Fears

* Afraid of being seen as the one who always brings up money.
* Afraid of logging an expense incorrectly and causing a dispute.
* Afraid the app will show outdated data.

---

=== Day-in-the-Life (First Person)

I got back from the grocery store and spent about $45 on shared
items, things for the kitchen that all three of us use. I open the
app and log it as a shared expense. I pick the equal split since we
all use the same stuff. Takes me about thirty seconds.

Later that evening my roommate texts asking if we are close to the
budget limit this month. I open the app and check we have used
about 80% of the monthly budget. I screenshot it and send it to the
group chat.

At the end of the month I look at the breakdown. I logged the most
expenses but that is just because I am the one who usually does the
grocery run. The totals look right.

---

=== Attitude Toward Tasks

Sofia approaches budget tracking pragmatically. She does not enjoy
the process of logging expenses, she sees it as a necessary habit,
not something she looks forward to. She will use the app consistently
only if it is fast and gives her information she cannot easily get
elsewhere. If logging an expense takes too long or the summary is
hard to read, she will stop using it.

She is comfortable with technology but not forgiving of confusing
interfaces. She expects the app to be obvious if she has to think
about what a button does, she considers that a design failure.

---

=== Open-Ended Questions Sofia Would Ask

* "How do I know if this expense was already logged by someone else?"
* "Can I change the split after I have already saved the expense?"
* "What happens to the budget if we change the limit in the middle
  of the month?"
* "Who can see my individual expenses, just the household, or
  anyone?"

---

=== Content and Communication Preferences

* *Preferred channel:* Mobile app, push notifications for budget
  alerts
* *Tone:* Direct and brief, she does not want explanations, she
  wants numbers
* *Style:* Visual summaries preferred over lists of transactions
* *Voice:* Neutral and informative, she does not want the app to
  feel like it is judging her spending

---

== What Sofia Tells the Team

Sofia's persona surfaces several concerns that are easy to miss when
thinking about the feature abstractly:

* The household summary must always reflect the latest data, stale
  summaries break trust immediately.
* Expense logging must be fast and forgiving, errors need to be
  correctable without friction.
* Split mode selection must be clear at the point of logging, Sofia
  should not have to remember what each mode means.
* Privacy within the household matters, Sofia wants to know who can
  see what before she trusts the system with her financial data.
