=== Evidence Concept Analysis

This subsection documents how raw observations from the rough sketch were transformed into structured domain concepts, ensuring traceability between the initial notes and the final abstractions used in the domain model.

==== Rough Sketch vs Concept Formation

[cols="1,1"]
|===
| Rough Sketch Statement | Derived Concept

| "All participants said they have at some point forgotten what they already had at home." (Brainstorming session)
| Household Item

| "Milk is usually bought twice in the same week at least once per month." (Shared apartment interview)
| Duplicate purchase / Coordination failure

| "Yogurts sometimes expire; estimated 3–5 thrown away last semester." (Solo student interview)
| Expiration Date

| "when we open the backup." / "We have rice in two places." (Shared apartment interview)
| Backup supply (multiple instances of a Household Item)

| "toilet paper runs out unexpectedly when guests visit." (Household owner interview)
| Availability (qualitative state: full, almost empty, backup) / Low-stock judgment
|===

Analyzing these side-by-side: different expressions for "groceries", "items", and "products" are unified under *Household Item*; multiple physical units become *Item Instances* (an "extra one" is a *Backup supply*); "expires" introduces *Expiration Date* tied to specific instances; and "running out" maps to *Availability* (with qualitative judgments: full, almost empty, backup). The resulting relationships and standardized terms are carried by the parent concept analysis and the terminology glossary, so they are not restated here.

==== Domain Scope Note

This analysis focuses strictly on domain concepts and avoids system-specific terminology — domain roles such as household members, roommates, or students are implied rather than "users". It highlights a representative subset of decisions: the parent concept analysis derives roughly 15 distinct concepts; this section traces 5 foundational ones to demonstrate the methodology of mapping raw observations to domain abstractions.
