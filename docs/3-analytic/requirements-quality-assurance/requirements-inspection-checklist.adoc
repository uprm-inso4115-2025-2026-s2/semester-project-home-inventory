== Requirements Inspection Checklist (Defect-Driven)

This checklist standardizes requirement reviews so the team can detect common defects early and record them consistently.

=== Purpose

Use this checklist to review requirement statements, identify defects, classify their severity, and assign follow-up actions.

=== How to Use

. Select the requirement section to review.
. Check each requirement against the defect categories below.
. Record findings in the review log.
. Classify each issue as Critical, Major, or Minor.
. Propose a concrete fix and assign an owner.

=== Severity Guide

* *Critical* — blocks implementation or testing, or may cause major incorrect behavior.
* *Major* — creates significant ambiguity, inconsistency, or traceability problems.
* *Minor* — reduces clarity or structure but does not block progress.

=== Defect-Driven Checklist

==== Omissions
* Is every requirement defined somewhere?
* Are expected inputs, outputs, and system responses covered?
* Are edge cases such as missing data, invalid values, or no response addressed?

==== Contradictions
* Does the requirement conflict with another requirement, goal, or constraint?
* Are terms used consistently across documents?

==== Inadequacies
* Does the requirement reflect a real stakeholder need?
* Is it valid for realistic student or roommate usage scenarios?

==== Ambiguities
* Can the statement be interpreted in more than one way?
* Are vague terms such as "fast", "easy", or "soon" replaced with measurable criteria?

==== Unmeasurability
* Does the requirement include fit criteria such as time, threshold, count, or accuracy?
* Is there a clear pass/fail condition?

==== Noise
* Is the statement relevant to system goals or constraints?
* Would removing it change expected system behavior or quality?

==== Overspecification
* Does the requirement force a design decision too early?
* Could the same goal be satisfied in more than one way?

==== Poor Structuring
* Is the requirement located in the correct section?
* Is it mixed with assumptions, domain properties, or implementation details?

==== Opacity and Modifiability
* Are dependencies between requirements explicit?
* Would changing this requirement force scattered edits across many documents?

=== Minimum Team Usage Rule

* Each team should apply this checklist at least once per sprint to active requirements.
* Findings should be linked to GitHub issues or PR comments for traceability.

=== Applied Inspection — Milestone 3

This section records the *actual* application of the checklist above to the project's requirement set, not just the framework. Every entry in <<applied-inspection-review-log>> is an inspection finding produced by walking the requirements against the defect categories.

==== Scope

Inspected:

* *Domain Requirements*: <<_domain_requirements,DR-1 through DR-25>>.
* *Interface Requirements*: <<_interface_requirements,IR-01 through IR-15>> and the deferred edge-case set IR-EC-A through IR-EC-I.
* *Machine Requirements*: <<_machine_requirements,MR-01 through MR-07>>.

==== Method

. The Requirements walkthroughs in <<_requirements_walkthroughs>> were re-read and every `[REQ-GAP]` / `[REQ-FUZZY]` finding was re-classified against the defect categories above (Omissions, Contradictions, Ambiguities, Unmeasurability, Noise, Overspecification, Poor Structuring, Opacity / Modifiability). The walkthroughs produce *execution-driven* findings — what fails when a real scenario is run. Re-classifying them through the inspection checklist produces *defect-typed* findings that map to the same fixes.
. Each requirement that was not exercised by a walkthrough was inspected directly against the checklist; this surfaced findings that the scenario approach could not have caught (e.g., wording contradictions between MR-05 and the canonical terminology, unmeasurability inside DR-12, opacity of DR-24's dependencies).
. Findings were assigned severity per the *Severity Guide*: *Critical* (blocks implementation/testing), *Major* (significant ambiguity / inconsistency / traceability), *Minor* (clarity / structure).
. Each finding received an `RQA-###` identifier, a proposed fix, and an owner placeholder (a role rather than a specific person — assignment happens during sprint planning).

==== Findings by defect category

===== Omissions

Requirements set is silent on behavior the user stories or downstream requirements depend on.

* `RQA-001` — Post-save same-device update of the inventory list is in the US-INV-01 acceptance criteria ("After saving, the item appears in the inventory list without a manual refresh") but no DR / IR commits to it. MR-04 covers *cross-device* synchronization; the *same-device, post-local-save* case is uncovered. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-002` — DR-12 ends at "make this status visible." Auto-add to the *Restock list / planner* on the NORMAL→LOW_STOCK transition is presupposed by DR-24 but not required upstream. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-003` — IR-15 covers snooze for *restock entries*; alert snooze (named in DR-14 as a re-alerting trigger) has no IR. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-004` — Cross-view consistency of derived item-state (low-stock, near-expiry, expired) across inventory list, *Restock list / planner*, and dashboard is in the US-ALT-01 acceptance criteria but no DR formalizes it. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-005` — DR-18 / IR-09 cover the *change* case for personal↔shared visibility but not the *initial-creation* case. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-006` — No IR dedicated to the *ownership-modification workflow*. IR-03 covers marking; the decomposed user story US-INV-02B has no requirement fulfilling it. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-007` — No DR/IR specifies what happens to *historical* views (spending history, past dashboards) when an item transitions personal↔shared. *Severity: Minor.*
* `RQA-008` — No DR/IR specifies the cost-split algorithm beyond "default equal, adjustable." Rounding rule and treatment of mid-cycle membership changes are unhandled. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-009` — No IR for editing an existing *Spending entry*. Edge-case IRs (IR-EC-D, IR-EC-E) hint at adjustment / refund but are deferred. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-010` — DR-1 / DR-17 cover the *barcode-keyed* uniqueness invariant. Duplicate handling for manually-created item types *without a barcode* has no clean coverage; DR-15 normalization is the closest hook but is framed for detection, not for the manual-no-barcode case. *Severity: Major.*

===== Ambiguities

Statements can be read more than one way.

* `RQA-011` — Neither DR-1 nor IR-12 specifies what the system displays on a *successful* barcode match (prefill the full item type? show a confirmation? auto-add?). *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-012` — IR-06 says "line items (or the purchase) as shared vs personal." Per-line vs per-purchase granularity is left to implementation choice. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-013` — IR-07 says "indicate reimbursement status" — a display-only verb. The user story expects user input to *mark* something paid. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-014` — IR-03 says "provide a means to mark" — same display-leaning verb pattern as IR-07; stakeholder expectation in US-INV-02B is an explicit *edit* flow. *Severity: Major.*

===== Contradictions

Wording disagrees with other requirements or with the canonical terminology.

* `RQA-015` — MR-05 mentions "owner/member/viewer/admin test accounts" — treats *Owner* and *admin* as distinct roles. Post-deduplication, <<_terminology>> defines *Owner* as the canonical role with *admin* as a synonym. *Severity: Minor.*
* `RQA-016` — DR-19 uses "admin" while the canonical Requirements role name is *Owner*. Consistent with MR-05's wording but inconsistent with the terminology table. *Severity: Minor.*

===== Unmeasurability

Requirement lacks a fit criterion or pass/fail condition.

* `RQA-017` — DR-12: "make this status visible to household members" — no latency bound. MR-02 / MR-04 cover lookup and cross-device sync but neither attaches to DR-12's visibility promise directly. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-018` — IR-08 (dashboard summaries) and US-RPT-01 acceptance criterion ("update after changes are recorded and synchronized — no stale information") have no time bound; closest is MR-04's 5-second cross-device sync, which is not explicitly cited from IR-08. *Severity: Minor.*

===== Noise

Content that does not drive system behavior.

* `RQA-019` — DR-3's parenthetical example list "(delete/archive/merge/split/adjust)" mixes the rule with illustrative examples. The rule itself is clear; the example list belongs in a notes column to reduce sentence noise. *Severity: Minor.*

===== Overspecification

Forces a design decision earlier than necessary.

* `RQA-020` — DR-7 hard-codes "a short window (initially 30 seconds)" into a domain-language requirement. The "initially" hedge signals the team knows this is provisional; the value should still be reviewed once implementation data is available, and the constraint may belong in interface or machine requirements rather than domain. *Severity: Minor.*

===== Opacity and Modifiability

Inter-requirement dependencies are not made explicit.

* `RQA-021` — DR-24 (Restock auto-add override) depends on DR-12 (threshold detection) and DR-14 (deduplication) but does not reference either. A change to either upstream requirement would silently affect DR-24's intended behavior. *Severity: Major.*
* `RQA-022` — DR-22 (default warning levels at 80% and 100%) depends on IR-13 (budget alert preferences) for user-facing configurability; the linkage is implicit. *Severity: Minor.*

===== Poor Structuring

Requirement located ambiguously or mixed with other concerns.

* `RQA-023` — Interface Requirements has a `Milestone 3 Additional Interface Requirements` subsection holding IR-09..IR-15 separately from IR-01..IR-08, which appears in the main list. The split makes sense historically (milestone-driven addition) but readers looking at the IR set must know to read both. *Severity: Minor.*
