== Objective

This document maps the relationships between the core domain entities in the Digital Home Inventory System.

== Core Domain Entities

The primary domain entities stated in the project's documentation are:

* Household
* Household Member
* Membership
* Invitation
* Location
* Item Type
* Item Instance
* Category
* Inventory
* Shopping List
* Shopping List Item
* Spending Record
* Budget
* Alert or Notification

This is a direct reference to the entities.adoc and terminology.adoc documents.

== Relationship Explanations

=== Household and Household Members

The relationship between a household and a member is best represented through a membership concept, because participation may change over time.

=== Household and Invitations

A household may send many invitations, and each invitation may result in a new membership if accepted.

=== Household and Locations

Locations represent physical places in a household where item instances may be stored, such as a pantry, fridge, kitchen cabinet, bathroom shelf, or hallway bin. A location may contain many item instances, but each item instance should have one current storage location.

=== Inventory and Item Instances

The inventory represents the real collection of items currently present in the household. It is composed of item instances.

=== Item Types and Item Instances

An item type defines the general concept of an item, while an item instance represents a specific physical unit of that item. This distinction helps the system represent duplicates, backups, expiration dates, quantities, and different storage locations.

=== Categories and Item Types

Categories group item types into meaningful organizational units. This relationship supports filtering, reporting, spending analysis, and budget organization.

=== Household Members and Inventory Operations

Household members interact with inventory by performing actions such as adding, locating, updating, transferring, consuming, or retiring item instances. This relationship shows that inventory is dynamic and changes through household member actions.

=== Shopping Lists and Item Types

Shopping lists represent planned or suggested purchases. A shopping list contains shopping list items, and each shopping list item usually refers to an item type that should be purchased.

=== Alerts and Notifications

Alerts and notifications are triggered by inventory-related conditions, such as low stock, expiration, or missing items. Household members may receive these alerts and decide whether to update the count, add the item to a shopping list, ignore the alert, or take another action.

=== Spending Records and Household Members

A spending record represents a financial event related to a household purchase. A household member may record spending when they buy an item for the household.

=== Spending Records and Budgets

Budgets provide spending limits or planning guidelines for the household. Spending records contribute to budget evaluation.

=== Budget and Category

A budget may apply to a whole household or to a specific category. Category-based budgets allow the household to track spending for groups such as groceries, cleaning supplies, or shared essentials.

This relationship helps connect inventory organization with financial planning.
