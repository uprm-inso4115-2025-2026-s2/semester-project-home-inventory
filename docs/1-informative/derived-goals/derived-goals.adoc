== Derived Goals

=== Primary Derived Goals

Beyond the primary goal established by the current situation and needs, the project pursues several secondary outcomes that guide decision-making across milestones. One is to encourage more sustainable household routines by reducing avoidable waste over time—items expiring unused or repurchased unnecessarily—through better awareness of what is already on hand. Another is to improve adoption and continued use: the project should support habits that stay realistic under busy student schedules, so tracking essentials does not collapse after initial setup.

A further goal is to strengthen coordination and trust in shared households. Even when people share expenses and supplies, misunderstandings about availability and replenishment create friction; the project aims to reduce these breakdowns as a secondary social outcome. Where budget awareness is in scope, an additional goal is to help students reflect on spending patterns around essentials, supporting informed planning over reactive purchases.

Finally, the project has internal quality goals that support sustainable progress: maintaining stable terminology and consistent documentation so decisions stay understandable and traceable (reducing rework and concept drift), and emphasizing evidence-based development through validation and verification activities (personas, scenarios, walkthroughs, testing) to keep the team's understanding aligned with stakeholder reality.

=== Competing Goals and Resolutions

The derived goals reinforce one another in many ways but also create natural tensions that must be handled explicitly. Identifying them helps the team avoid inconsistent design decisions and produces derived requirements clarifying how the system should behave when important goals compete.

==== 1. Low-effort use vs. inventory accuracy and reliability

*Competing goal A:* Keep the system lightweight enough that students can realistically maintain it under busy schedules. +
*Competing goal B:* Preserve inventory information with enough accuracy and completeness for reminders, shared coordination, and planning to remain trustworthy.

These compete because reducing friction often means requiring less information at entry, but requiring too little makes the inventory incomplete, stale, or unreliable. The system must balance continued adoption with dependable data quality.

*Resolution policy:* The system should minimize required user effort during common workflows, but still collect and preserve the minimum information needed to support accurate inventory state and reliable behavior.

*Derived requirements:*

* `DG-REQ-01` Users shall be able to create an initial item record with only the minimum information needed to identify and track that item, while adding richer metadata later as needed.
* `DG-REQ-02` Low-stock and expiration-related behaviors shall only occur when the item information required to evaluate those conditions is present and valid.
* `DG-REQ-03` Recorded item metadata shall remain stable and trustworthy over time unless an authorized user explicitly changes or removes it.
* `DG-REQ-04` Routine item updates shall remain lightweight enough to fit ordinary student workflows without creating excessive maintenance burden.

*Trace hooks:* `N1`, `N2`, `N3`; derived goal for adoption and continued use; reliability quality need; `US-INV-01`, `US-INV-02`.

==== 2. Timely reminders vs. avoiding notification fatigue

*Competing goal A:* Ensure users are reminded in time about low-stock and expiration conditions. +
*Competing goal B:* Avoid excessive or poorly timed alerts that cause frustration, desensitization, or notification disablement.

These compete because a highly proactive reminder strategy reduces missed conditions but risks overwhelming users and undermining trust. The project includes automated low-stock reminders, expiration alerts, and customizable thresholds, while emphasizing that notifications stay helpful and relevant.

*Resolution policy:* The system should favor timely reminders for important conditions, but rate-limit and deduplicate recurring alerts so persistent states stay visible without becoming spam.

*Derived requirements:*

* `DG-REQ-05` Users shall receive low-stock reminders often enough to support timely replenishment, but not more than once per item for the same household member group within a single calendar day.
* `DG-REQ-06` Users shall receive expiration-related reminders often enough to support timely action, but not more than once per item for the same household member group within a single calendar day unless a new alert condition is created by a state change.
* `DG-REQ-07` Repeated sweep executions shall not produce duplicate notifications for the same item-condition pair within the same daily reminder period.
* `DG-REQ-08` Reminder behavior shall favor relevance and timeliness over notification volume by limiting prompts to conditions supported by valid item data and active alert rules.

*Trace hooks:* `N2`; usability and reliability quality needs; `Lightweight Smart Inventory`; `US-ALT-01`.

==== 3. Shared household visibility vs. privacy and least privilege

*Competing goal A:* Increase household coordination by making shared inventory visible to cohabitants. +
*Competing goal B:* Protect user-specific or sensitive information by exposing only what is necessary for coordination.

These compete because coordination improves when members can see what is available and what needs replenishment, but excessive visibility can expose ownership, spending, or personal-item information that not everyone should access. The system must support collaboration without ignoring access boundaries.

*Resolution policy:* The system should expose the minimum shared information required for household coordination while restricting user-specific metadata to the appropriate scope and permissions.

*Derived requirements:*

* `DG-REQ-09` Household members shall be able to see the availability and replenishment-relevant status of shared items.
* `DG-REQ-10` Shared household data and personal or user-specific item data shall remain distinguishable when both exist in the same household context.
* `DG-REQ-11` Ownership, spending, and other user-specific item information shall only be visible to users with permission to view it.
* `DG-REQ-12` The distinction between shared items and personal items shall remain clear whenever that distinction affects replenishment or household coordination.

*Trace hooks:* `N4`, `N5`; derived goal for shared-household coordination and trust; security/privacy quality need; `US-SB-01`.
