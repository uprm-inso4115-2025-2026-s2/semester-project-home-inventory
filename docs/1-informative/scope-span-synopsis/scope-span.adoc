== Scope and Span

*Scope*

The project's scope is to carry out a software engineering project in the domain of student household management. It investigates how students living independently (alone or with roommates) track household items and spending, and it produces a system-to-be plus the deliverables needed to build it responsibly (domain description, requirements, architecture/design, implementation, validation/verification, and documentation). It targets pain points caused by relying on memory and informal tracking, and supports both individual and shared-household workflows across these areas:

* Domain Engineering: Analyze how students currently manage household items, including breakdowns such as forgetting what is available, inconsistent updates, and poor roommate coordination. Identify the major actors (students living alone or with roommates/cohabitants) and domain concepts (items, categories, locations/rooms, quantities, thresholds, expiration dates, shared responsibility).

* Requirements Engineering: Define requirements supporting inventory accuracy, fast updates, and coordination — covering core inventory maintenance, item organization, lookup before purchasing, shared-household coordination, and budget awareness — while capturing constraints such as busy schedules, low tolerance for complex setup, and the need for privacy controls.

* Software Architecture: Develop a high-level architecture supporting mobile use, persistent storage, and shared-household synchronization, identifying major components and responsibilities (client, data storage, synchronization, authentication/authorization, notifications/reminders) with testability and maintainability in mind.

* Software Design: Design low-friction workflows for inventory maintenance (capture, update, lookup) that remain understandable in shared households, provide clear feedback when items are low, near-expiration, or shared, and present budget summaries that support planning rather than reactive purchasing.

* Software Process: Follow an Agile, feature-team structure where each team owns its feature end-to-end (research, design, implementation, testing, documentation). Work is tracked through a structured issue and pull-request workflow to keep changes traceable and reviewed, with continuous feedback from team walkthroughs and scenario reviews guiding refinement.

*Span*

The project's span focuses on typical student living environments (dorms, apartments, shared houses) where household management is informal and time-constrained. The initial span targets small households with a modest inventory and few cohabitants, assuming frequent lightweight updates rather than occasional heavy maintenance.

* Specifics of the System: Users maintain a centralized inventory organized by room and category, update quantities, and optionally track expiration dates and priorities. Members choose how they are informed about low-stock and upcoming expirations. Shared households view a common inventory and distinguish shared from personal items to reduce duplicates and conflict. Budget tracking connects purchases to categories and provides basic spending summaries and reports.

* Target Audience and Expansion: The initial audience is students living independently, including those with roommates. Expansion may include richer analytics (usage rates and trends), more advanced reports, optional integrations with external productivity or budgeting tools, and improved restock-planning automation — always centered on student needs and low-friction maintenance.

* Methodology and Maintenance: The project proceeds iteratively, refining scope, requirements, documentation, and implementation through cycles of validation and verification as stakeholder feedback and early testing reveal gaps, ensuring the system remains practical for real student workflows and constraints.
