== Current Situation

Students living independently often lose track of household items such as groceries, cleaning supplies, toiletries, and medicine. Tracking is usually ad-hoc (memory, notes, receipts, or messages), so information becomes outdated quickly, and students buy duplicates, run out of essentials unexpectedly, or discover expired items during cleanup. In shared households the problem is worse: roommates lack visibility into what is already available, increasing duplicate purchases and making budgeting harder, since there is no consistent shared tracking method across people and time.

=== Domain context
Although several home inventory applications exist, most target insurance documentation or business inventory management rather than lightweight everyday use. The tools students try (notes, basic lists, or heavier inventory apps) are either too informal to stay accurate over time or too cumbersome for daily tracking, so shared inventory information quickly becomes unreliable.

=== Who is involved (actors)
 * Primary actors: Students and young adults managing personal household items
 * Secondary actors: Roommates or cohabitants sharing supplies
 * Organizations/systems involved: Existing home inventory mobile applications (e.g., Sortly, PantryCheck)


=== Current workflow (as-is)
. User purchases or owns household items
. User optionally records items manually in notes or an inventory app
. Inventory is rarely updated consistently over time
. User forgets what items are already available
. User repurchases duplicate or unnecessary items
. In shared households, roommates lack visibility into available supplies

=== Pain points
[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Pain point |Who experiences it |Evidence / example

|Too much of a hassle when adding items
|Students and casual users
|User reviews report that manual item entry consumes too much time and discourages continued use.

|Inventory becomes outdated quickly
|Almost all users
|Many users abandon inventory apps after initial setup because maintenance takes too long.

|Excessive or poorly timed notifications
|Mobile users
|Reviews indicate too many notifications, leading users to disable alerts, defeating one of the apps' main purposes.

|Pricing perceived as too high for casual use
|Students and young adults
|Premium tiers are designed for business users and lock features that individuals could use.

|Lack of effective shared household inventory support
|Roommates and cohabitants
|Most apps offer limited or no real-time collaboration, making it hard for multiple members to maintain one accurate inventory, which causes miscommunication or duplicate purchases.
|===


=== Consequences
* Time/cost impact: Users waste money on duplicate purchases and spend extra time checking inventory manually.
* Quality/accuracy impact: When tracking relies on abandoned or partially used tools, the record becomes incomplete and untrusted.
* User experience impact: Frustration leads to abandonment of existing apps.
* Organizational impact: Shared households experience coordination inefficiencies.


=== Assumptions and constraints (as-is)
* Assumption: Target users prefer low-effort mobile workflows.
* Assumption: Users are more likely to maintain inventory if entry is fast and simple.
* Constraint: Users have limited patience for manual data entry.
* Constraint: The solution must work well on mobile devices with intermittent connectivity.
