== Acquisition Plan

This section defines how raw domain knowledge and evidence are gathered before being interpreted into stakeholder needs and requirements (the acquisition/elicitation distinction is established in the parent section above).

=== Goals
* Capture real household practices instead of idealized descriptions.
* Understand shared-household coordination (visibility, duplicates, ownership, cost splitting).
* Identify friction points that cause tracking methods to be abandoned.
* Clarify edge cases such as bulk purchases, multiple locations, and missing expiration information.
* Build a traceable base of raw domain evidence for later elicitation.

=== Methods

These methods gather raw domain data before formal analysis or requirement extraction:

* *Questionnaire:* short questions collecting recurring household practices and pain points across participants.
* *Interviews (4–6):* semi-structured interviews with solo students and students in shared households, including at least one student athlete.
* *Contextual walkthroughs:* participants demonstrate current tracking methods and explain their shorthand and terminology.
* *Item analysis:* review of receipts, pantry/fridge photos, and shared shopping messages to understand how items are tracked or forgotten.
* *Light competitive scan:* review a small set of existing apps for repeated friction points such as entry effort and notification fatigue.

[NOTE]
====
Broader surveys and larger quantitative validation may be deferred if time is limited. This milestone prioritizes qualitative evidence supporting domain understanding and early requirements work.
====

=== Risks and Mitigations
* *Risk:* Participants are too busy for long interviews. — *Mitigation:* keep sessions short and allow asynchronous follow-up.
* *Risk:* Participants describe ideal rather than actual behavior. — *Mitigation:* ask for recent concrete examples and current artifacts (notes, chats, receipts).
* *Risk:* Manual-entry tolerance is very low. — *Mitigation:* ask explicitly what minimum information participants will record.
* *Risk:* Acquisition and elicitation are mixed too early. — *Mitigation:* record raw evidence first, then interpret it in a separate elicitation step.

== Second-Round Requirements Acquisition

This second round focused on gaps and ambiguities found during implementation, clarifying implementation-driven uncertainties around shared-household visibility, role-based permissions, budget alerts, search/filter behavior, barcode fallback, notification fatigue, grocery-list automation, and offline synchronization. It simulated short follow-up interviews and lightweight walkthroughs with ChatGPT acting as a participant, showing current app behaviors and asking targeted questions about ambiguous cases.

| M3-QA-02
| High
| What should viewer, member, and admin roles be allowed to do?

| DR-19, IR-09, MR-05

| M3-QA-03
| Medium
| Which filters are essential when checking inventory at the store?
| M3-P02 prioritized search by name, shared/personal ownership, and low/out-of-stock status; M3-P03 prioritized location and category; M3-P01 wanted "expiring soon" visible without searching. Owner/location/status filters mattered more than advanced date-range search.
| Lookup must support minimum filters for shopping/planning: name, location, category, ownership/scope, stock/expiration status.
| DR-20, IR-10, US-RPT-01

| M3-QA-04
| High
| When barcode lookup fails, what is the minimum acceptable manual-entry flow?
| M3-P01 and M3-P04 would abandon the workflow if too many fields were required. Minimum acceptable: item name and quantity/availability; location and expiration optional or editable later. M3-P03 wanted to save quickly and complete details afterward.
| Barcode fallback must not block capture; manual entry allows a minimum viable record, with richer metadata added later.
| DR-21, IR-11, US-INV-01, MR-08

| M3-QA-05
| Medium
| Do users want budget alerts, and how should they be delivered?
| M3-P02 wanted warnings around 80% and 100% of a weekly budget; M3-P03 preferred dashboard/in-app indicators over push. Budget alerts should be opt-in since not every household tracks spending strictly.
| Budget alerts should be optional, threshold-based, and in-app by default, with push only by user preference.
| DR-22, IR-12, US-SB-01, US-RPT-01

| M3-QA-06
| High
| When a shared item is low, expired, or out of stock, who should receive the alert?
| M3-P02 and M3-P03: everyone should see the alert in-app, but push should not go to every member every time. M3-P05 preferred grouped daily summaries. Participants rejected repeated alerts for unchanged conditions.
| Shared alerts create one household-level record, visible in-app, with push/digest delivery controlled by preference and deduplication.
| DR-23, IR-13, MR-07

| M3-QA-07
| Medium
| Should auto-added grocery-list items be removable or snoozable?
| M3-P03 liked auto-add but wanted to remove/snooze irrelevant items; M3-P02 said the system should not immediately re-add a removed item unless quantity/status changes again.
| Auto-added restock items must stay user-controllable; removing/snoozing suppresses re-addition until a new qualifying transition or the snooze expires.

| Medium
| Is offline viewing enough, or are offline updates needed?

| Offline support revised from view-only to queued pending updates for item creation and quantity changes, with clear sync status and conflict handling.

=== Outcome

The acquisition round produced these requirement decisions:

* Personal items are private by default unless explicitly shared.
* Household roles must define clear permissions for viewing, editing, inviting, removing, and administering shared inventory.
* Store-use lookup requires practical filters: name, location, category, ownership/scope, stock status, expiration status.
* Barcode fallback must support fast manual capture with minimal required fields.
* Budget alerts should be opt-in, threshold-based, and in-app by default.
* Shared alerts should be household-level records with configurable delivery, not repeated push to every member.
* Auto-added grocery-list entries must support manual removal or snooze without immediate re-addition.
* Offline support should include pending item creation and quantity updates, not only view-only access.

Each requirement added or revised from this round is marked in Section 2.2.
