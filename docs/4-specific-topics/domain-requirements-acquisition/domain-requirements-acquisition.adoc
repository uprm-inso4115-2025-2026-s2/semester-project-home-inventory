== Domain Requirements Acquisition

This section documents how raw domain evidence was gathered and later used during elicitation, keeping the two activities distinct. *Acquisition* collects raw domain material before it is interpreted — questionnaires, short interviews, contextual walkthroughs, and examples of current household tracking practices. *Elicitation* begins after acquisition: grouping repeated problems, identifying needs and conflicts, and transforming evidence into stories and requirements.

=== Questionnaire

A short questionnaire collected recurring household practices, coordination problems, and tracking pain points before deeper interviews, focusing on current behavior rather than desired features. Sample items:

* What kinds of household items do you most often lose track of?
* How do you currently keep track of what you have at home?
* Can you describe a recent time when you bought something you already had?
* How do you usually tell that an item is running low?
* Do you think in exact quantities or rough judgments like "almost empty" or "backup left"?
* Do you ever store the same kind of item in multiple places?
* Have you ever thrown something away because it expired or was forgotten?
* If you live with others, how do you coordinate who buys shared items?
* What kinds of reminders would be helpful, and what kinds would feel annoying?
* What would make inventory tracking fast enough that you would actually keep using it?

=== Interview Protocol

Short semi-structured interviews followed the questionnaire to obtain specific stories and clarify ambiguous responses:

. assign a participant code before taking notes
. begin with current tracking habits
. ask for one or two recent concrete stories
. probe for details (item, location, what the participant believed, what actually happened)
. ask about edge cases (duplicate purchases, expired items, multiple locations, guests, move-out)
. summarize the main points back to confirm understanding

Example prompts:

* "Tell me about the last time you bought something and later realized you already had it."
* "Walk me through a specific time when you ran out of an item unexpectedly."
* "When you say an item was 'almost empty', what made you think that?"
* "Can you describe a roommate situation that caused confusion about a shared item?"

=== Participant Coding

To preserve anonymity while tracing statements from the same participant consistently, participants are identified by stable codes (quotes, notes, and walkthrough findings refer to codes, not real names):

* P01 — student living alone
* P02 — student in shared apartment
* P03 — student in shared apartment
* P04 — student athlete
* P05 — shared-household organizer

=== From Acquisition to Elicitation

After acquisition, raw answers are reviewed and grouped into recurring domain problems and concepts, then used during elicitation to derive needs, stories, and requirements:

. collect raw questionnaire and interview evidence
. group repeated observations and pain points
. identify the related domain concept or conflict
. derive needs, candidate requirements, or open questions
. link results back to the source evidence
