== Narrative

A cohesive story of how the domain works, independent of any specific software system.

=== Overview

Students living independently go through a continuous cycle of acquiring, using, and replacing household items: they buy food and supplies when they have time and money, store them in their living space, and consume them gradually. When items run low or out, someone must notice and replenish them. In shared households this cycle involves multiple members who must coordinate—or fail to coordinate—about what is needed, who will buy it, and when.

Tracking is typically informal, relying on memory, a fridge note, a chat message, or a quick pantry check. This understanding can diverge from reality: items may exist but be forgotten in another location, backups may be opened without being recorded, and purchases may be duplicated from incomplete information. Waste occurs when items expire unnoticed or duplicates are bought while another instance is still available. Consumption is not stable either—during exam weeks or guest visits items are used faster—and bulk purchases can introduce many instances at once, sometimes with different expiration dates in a single pack.

Some households also keep an informal sense of spending on essentials, remembered loosely or estimated from receipts. When tracked, even imperfectly, spending influences decisions such as buying in bulk for a lower unit price, delaying non-urgent restocks, or choosing cheaper alternatives. In shared households, disputes arise when it is unclear which purchases are personal versus shared or when one member repeatedly pays for shared items. Budget awareness therefore becomes another dimension of household coordination alongside availability and replenishment.

=== Actors and artifacts

==== Actors (domain roles):
* Students living alone (single-member household)
* Students living with roommates/cohabitants (multi-member household)
* Household members who often coordinate replenishment (informal "household manager" role)
* Household members who focus on budget awareness (budget-conscious role)

==== Artifacts used today (domain-visible):
* Fridge notes / whiteboards
* Chat messages (e.g., group chat)
* Notes app lists
* Pantry photos
* Receipts or memory-based estimates

=== Typical domain flow (as observed)

. Household members acquire items (sometimes in bulk) and store them in household locations.
. Items are consumed over time; availability decreases.
. A member checks availability using informal artifacts (note, memory, pantry check).
. If an item is judged low or out (often qualitatively—"feels light", "opened backup"), replenishment is triggered.
. In shared households, coordination may succeed (one person buys) or fail (duplicate buying).
. Some items expire before use; households may discover this late and discard them.
. Members may keep informal awareness of spending; when tracked, it influences replenishment choices and can create shared-vs-personal disputes.
. Membership changes (move-out) require updating who participates and who is informed.

=== #Scenario-Based Domain Interactions#

The following vignettes illustrate domain interactions. The personas they reference (Mateo, Sofia, Julian, Andrea) are detailed in Section 4.2.

* #*Monthly pantry audit (Mateo, solo).* Auditing his pantry, fridge, and hallway cabinet before shopping, Mateo finds expired yogurt pushed behind newer groceries and a duplicate ketchup bottle he bought because he forgot to check another location. He updates his restock list to only what is truly needed — showing how incomplete awareness, forgotten locations, expiration, and replenishment decisions emerge under time constraints.#
* #*Duplicate shared purchase (Sofia, shared household).* Sofia checks the shared inventory, sees paper towels and dish soap are low, and buys replacements — only to find a roommate had already bought bulk paper towels that afternoon without communicating it. The inventory became inconsistent because members replenished independently from incomplete information, illustrating synchronization failure and duplicate purchasing.#
* #*Specialty stockout and expiry (Julian, dietary needs).* Julian finds the last box of his preferred gluten-free pasta was consumed days earlier with no replenishment, and that several bulk specialty items expired before use because quantities and dates were tracked inconsistently — showing why threshold awareness, expiration management, and replenishment timing matter for specialized dependencies.#
* #*Lending a household tool (Andrea, organizer).* Andrea marks the household drill temporarily unavailable when lending it to a neighbor; a roommate later learns from the records that it is outside the household, and Andrea restores it on return — showing how the inventory supports temporary transfers, shared visibility, and location awareness.#
