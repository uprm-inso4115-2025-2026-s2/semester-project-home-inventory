== Terminology

This section consolidates the domain's ubiquitous language into a single annotated reference. Every term is tagged with the phase in which it was first introduced:

* *Domain* — emerged from domain analysis (interviews, observations, informal practices)
* *Requirements* — introduced when specifying capabilities of the system-to-be
* *Implementation* — introduced during M2/M3 software design, architecture, and deployment work

[cols="2,1,1,4,3", options="header"]
|===
| Term
| Type
| Phase
| Definition
| Synonyms / Notes

| Household
| Entity
| Domain
| A living unit (dorm, apartment, shared house) where one or more people manage household essentials.
| Previously "Home."

| Household member
| Entity
| Domain
| A person who belongs to a household and participates in consuming, replenishing, or coordinating around items.
| Previously "Owner" or "Occupant."

| Invitation
| Entity / Event
| Domain
| A temporary request that allows a participant to join a shared household context.
| Important states may include sent, accepted, declined, expired, or revoked.

| Membership
| Relationship / Concept
| Domain
| The relationship connecting a participant to a household for shared coordination and participation.
| Changes over time through joining, leaving, removal, or invitation acceptance.

| Location
| Entity
| Domain
| A storage place within a household (kitchen cabinet, pantry area, bathroom closet, car trunk, hallway bin).
| Previously "Room."

| Item type
| Entity
| Domain
| The kind of an item, independent of a specific package (e.g., "Milk", "Dish soap", "Toilet paper").
| Previously "Product." "Asset" and "Belonging" should be avoided unless referring generally to household possessions; use "Item type" for the kind of item and "Item instance" for a specific physical package.

| Item instance (package)
| Entity
| Domain
| A concrete package or unit of an item type that physically exists in the household (e.g., a specific milk carton).
| Previously "Stock." "Asset" and "Belonging" may appear in older drafts, but "Item instance" is the preferred term for a specific physical object/package.

| Inventory (reality)
| Entity
| Domain
| The set of item instances that physically exist in the household at a given time.
| Real-world state, not a list or record.

| Tracked inventory
| Concept
| Domain
| The household's collective view of what is on hand, as represented through tracked inventory records rather than direct observation. It is the system-facing counterpart to *Inventory (reality)* and is what household members consult when they cannot physically check a location. Tracked inventory may diverge from reality whenever a consumption, restock, or discard event occurs without being recorded.
| Used as shorthand for the union of all tracked inventory records for a household. Use *Tracked inventory record* when referring to one specific entry, and *Inventory (reality)* when referring to the actual physical state.

| Spending category
| Entity / Label
| Domain
| A grouping for spending (e.g., groceries, cleaning, personal care). May align with item categories, but is not required to.
| —

| Budget
| Entity / Rule
| Domain
| A financial limit defined for a period (household-level or category-level) used to evaluate spending.
| Budget window and cycle can vary.

| Tracked inventory record
| Artifact
| Domain
| A representation of availability used by household members (fridge note, list, memory, chat message). May diverge from reality.
| Supports the "tracked vs. reality" distinction.

| Restock list / planner
| Artifact
| Domain
| A household-oriented list of items that should be replenished soon (fridge note, notes-app list, chat).
| Canonical domain term. "Grocery list" is the system-facing feature name when the replenishment list is presented through the application.

| Alert / notification (domain-level)
| Artifact / Event
| Domain
| A message that informs a household member about an actionable condition (low-stock, out-of-stock, expiring, expired, budget threshold crossed).
| General notification concept. "Duplicate warning" is a specialized alert used for possible duplicate item or purchase situations.

| Spending entry
| Record
| Domain
| A record of a financial transaction in a household (purchase, refund, or adjustment), optionally associated with an item type and spending category.
| Supports budgeting and reporting.

| Transaction (quantity change)
| Record
| Domain
| A record of an availability change (consume, restock, or adjust) with a time and reason.
| Supports traceability and verification.

| Availability
| Property
| Domain
| A household's current understanding of how much of an item is left for use. Availability may be expressed qualitatively ("full", "almost empty", "backup opened"), quantitatively as a *Quantity*, or as a system-recognized *Actionable state* (e.g., LOW_STOCK, OUT_OF_STOCK). It describes what household members believe is on hand, which may differ from the *Inventory (reality)*.
| Distinct from *Quantity* (a numeric measure) and *Actionable state* (a system-computed condition). Availability is the broader human-facing concept; the other two are specific representations of it.

| Quantity
| Property
| Domain
| A numeric measure of availability for an item type or item instance, expressed as a count (e.g., 3 cartons) or in units (e.g., 500 g). May be exact, approximate, or unknown, but never negative. Quantity is the precise representation of *Availability* that the system uses to evaluate thresholds and detect actionable states.
| Distinct from *Availability* (which may be qualitative) and *Low-stock threshold* / *Reorder point* (which are the boundaries quantity is compared against).

| Expiration date
| Property
| Domain
| A date after which an item instance is considered expired or undesirable. Applies to some items, not all; may be unreadable or missing.
| —

| Low-stock threshold
| Rule / Property
| Domain
| A boundary value used to decide when an item is considered low.
| May be a fixed quantity initially.

| Budget cycle (period)
| Concept
| Domain
| The time window over which spending is compared to a budget (weekly, monthly, custom). Resets and rollovers are policy decisions.
| —

| Backup supply
| Practice / Concept
| Domain
| A household practice of keeping a spare instance or package to reduce the risk of running out.
| Often used instead of numeric tracking.

| Consumption
| Action
| Domain
| Using an item over time, reducing its availability. Rate may vary by context (exam weeks, guests).
| —

| Replenishment (restock)
| Action
| Domain
| Acquiring more of an item, increasing availability. Often triggered after low-stock, out-of-stock, or expiration events.
| —

| Snooze / defer
| Action
| Domain
| A household member delays acting on an alert for a period of time.
| The household member may receive another alert for the same actionable condition later.

| Dismiss / ignore
| Action
| Domain
| A household member acknowledges an alert and chooses not to act on it.
| —

| Low-stock judgment
| Event / Rule
| Domain
| A decision that an item is running low, based on a household strategy ("feels light", "opened backup") or a numeric threshold.
| Not inherently numeric.

| Out-of-stock
| State / Event
| Domain
| The condition or event where an item is considered unavailable because it is gone (quantity at zero, or judged empty).
| Distinct from low-stock.

| Near-expiry
| State / Event
| Domain
| An item instance approaching its expiration date within a defined window (e.g., N days).
| Window length can vary.

| Expired
| State / Event
| Domain
| An item instance past its expiration date.
| Expired does not always mean unavailable; policy varies by household.

| Budget threshold crossing
| Event
| Domain
| The moment when spending reaches or exceeds a defined threshold (e.g., 80%, 100% of the budget).
| Can trigger alerts, subject to anti-spam rules and the budget cycle.

| Item acquired
| Event
| Domain
| A household member obtains an item instance (possibly in bulk) and brings it into the household.
| "Bought 24 yogurts from Costco."

| Item stored
| Event
| Domain
| An item instance is placed in a household location.
| "Rice stored in kitchen cabinet; backup in hallway bin."

| Item consumed
| Event
| Domain
| A household member uses an item, decreasing its availability.
| Usage rate may increase during exams or when guests visit. "Consumption rate" is treated as a synonym of "usage rate."

| Backup opened
| Event
| Domain
| A household member begins using the backup supply of an item.
| Often used as a qualitative low-stock signal.

| Replenishment decided
| Event
| Domain
| A household member decides the household should acquire more of an item type.
| May be prompted by low-stock, out-of-stock, or expiration.

| Item replenished
| Event
| Domain
| New item instances of an item type are acquired to restore availability.
| Can be duplicated if coordination fails.

| Duplicate purchase
| Event
| Domain
| More of an item type is acquired unnecessarily because household members acted on inconsistent information.
| Common for milk, eggs, and rice per interviews.

| Item approaching expiration
| Event
| Domain
| An item instance is within a near-expiry window.
| Motivates timely use or discard decisions.

| Item expired
| Event
| Domain
| An item instance passes its expiration date (if it has one).
| Policy on what to do varies by item type and household.

| Item discarded
| Event
| Domain
| A household member throws away an item instance (expired, spoiled, or unwanted).
| Can occur even when another unopened instance exists.

| Alert generated
| Event
| Domain
| A low-stock, out-of-stock, near-expiry, expired, or budget condition becomes actionable and is communicated to household members.
| —

| Alert dismissed / deferred
| Event
| Domain
| A household member acknowledges an alert and chooses to ignore it or act later.
| Supports snooze and anti-spam behavior.

| Restock entry created / updated
| Event
| Domain
| An item is added to or updated on the restock list due to low-stock, out-of-stock, or expired conditions.
| May be manual or derived from judgments.

| Spending recorded
| Event
| Domain
| A household member logs a spending transaction (purchase, refund, or adjustment).
| May be shared or personal.

| Budget cycle started / ended
| Event
| Domain
| A new budget period begins or a period closes.
| Supports reset and rollover policies.

| Household membership changed
| Event
| Domain
| A member moves in or out; household participation changes.
| Motivates access removal expectations later.

| User
| Actor
| Requirements
| A person who interacts with the system-to-be through its interface. Corresponds to a household member in the domain.
| "User" is the system-facing term; "Household member" is the domain-facing term.

| Household admin
| Role
| Requirements
| The single household member with administrative responsibility at a given time. Transferable before the admin leaves.
| Domain equivalent: informal "household manager" role.

| Membership state
| Property
| Requirements
| The status of a user's participation in a household (active, pending, revoked).
| No direct domain artifact.

| Reorder point (threshold)
| Rule / Property
| Requirements
| A quantity value at or below which an item type is considered low. Initial requirements may accept a fixed per-item threshold.
| Refines the domain's "low-stock threshold" with a numeric constraint.

| Near-expiry window
| Rule / Property
| Requirements
| The definition of "expiring soon" — the number of days before expiration that triggers a near-expiry alert.
| Window value is a requirements decision deferred from the domain.

| Deduplication / anti-spam rule
| Rule
| Requirements
| A constraint that prevents the system from creating another alert or auto-added restock entry for the same household, item, condition type, and triggering state while that condition remains unchanged. A repeated scheduled check, page refresh, or synchronization event is not enough to create a duplicate. A new alert or restock entry is allowed only when the underlying actionable state changes, the user explicitly snoozes/clears the previous entry and the snooze period expires, or the condition is resolved and later becomes true again.
| Operationalizes the domain concern that repeated checks should not create alert spam or duplicate restock work.

| Actionable state
| Concept
| Requirements
| A system-recognized condition (low-stock, out-of-stock, near-expiry, expired, budget-threshold-crossed) that may trigger an alert or restock entry.
| Refines domain-level events into system-detectable states.

| Budget status (derived)
| Concept
| Requirements
| A computed view of spending versus budget within a cycle: amount spent, amount remaining, percentage used, and threshold flags.
| Derived from spending entries; not directly observable in the domain.

| Scheduled sweep
| Function
| Requirements
| A periodic background check that evaluates low-stock and expiration conditions without requiring the user to open the system.
| Automation mechanism; delivery details are implementation-level.

| Notification record
| Record
| Requirements
| A persisted representation of an alert, including type, timestamp, and read/unread status, available at minimum for in-app review.
| Refines the domain-level "alert"; adds persistence and status tracking.

| Barcode scanning
| Function
| Requirements
| A system feature that allows a user to add or identify an item by scanning its barcode instead of entering all item details manually.
| Related to manual entry and item acquisition.

| Manual entry
| Function
| Requirements
| A system feature that allows a user to add or update item information by typing the details directly into the application.
| Used when barcode scanning is unavailable, unreliable, or the item has no barcode.

| Shared inventory
| Concept
| Requirements
| An inventory context where multiple household members can view, coordinate, and update item-related information.
| Related to household membership, permission rules, and duplicate purchase prevention.

| Permission rule
| Rule
| Requirements
| A constraint that defines what actions a user or household member is allowed to perform within a household or shared inventory.
| Examples include viewing items, editing items, inviting members, removing members, or managing shared household data.

| Grocery list
| Artifact
| Requirements
| A system-facing feature that presents items the user may need to purchase or replenish.
| Use "Restock list / planner" as the canonical domain term. Use "Grocery list" only when referring to the implemented or designed application feature.

| Duplicate warning
| Alert / Rule
| Requirements
| A specialized alert that informs the user when an item, purchase, or restock action may duplicate an existing inventory item or planned item.
| Specialized form of "Alert / notification"; supports duplicate purchase prevention and shared household coordination.

| Report
| Artifact
| Requirements
| A general system-generated summary that presents household information to support review or decision-making.
| Umbrella term only. Prefer more specific terms such as "Expenditure report" or "Stock summary" when the report type is known.

| Usage rate
| Derived Property
| Requirements
| A computed estimate of how quickly an item is consumed over time.
| Canonical term. "Consumption rate" is treated as a synonym and should be replaced with "usage rate" in running text when possible.

| Expenditure report
| Artifact
| Requirements
| A report that summarizes spending entries over a selected period, category, household, or user.
| Related to budget status, spending category, and spending entry.

| Stock summary
| Artifact
| Requirements
| A report or view that summarizes current item availability, low-stock items, out-of-stock items, and related inventory conditions.
| Related to availability, quantity, low-stock judgment, and actionable states.

| Item condition
| Property
| Domain
| The observed state of an item instance, such as new, opened, damaged, expired, spoiled, or usable.
| Helps household members decide whether an item should be kept, used, repaired, discarded, or replaced.

| Sentimental value
| Property
| Domain
| The personal or emotional importance assigned to an item beyond its practical or financial value.
| Important for non-consumable household possessions where disposal or replacement decisions are not based only on quantity or cost.

| Inventory operations
| Concept
| Domain
| The set of actions that change or inspect the household inventory, such as adding, locating, updating, transferring, consuming, replenishing, archiving, or discarding items.
| Umbrella term for inventory-related actions described across scenarios, requirements, and implementation sections.

| Supabase
| External Service
| Implementation
| The backend platform used by the system to support database persistence, authentication, storage, real-time synchronization, and backend functions when applicable.
| M2/M3 implementation and deployment term used across software design, architecture, and validation/verification documentation.

| Supabase Auth
| External Service / Authentication
| Implementation
| The authentication service used to identify users and provide session information for authorization decisions.
| Related to household membership, permission rules, RLS policies, and authenticated access.

| Supabase Realtime
| Integration Mechanism
| Implementation
| A Supabase service that allows the application to receive backend data updates without requiring users to manually refresh the interface.
| Used for shared inventory synchronization, dashboard updates, and multi-user coordination.

| Row Level Security (RLS)
| Security Mechanism
| Implementation
| A database-level access control mechanism used to restrict which records a user can view or modify based on household membership, role, or permission rules.
| Often abbreviated as "RLS"; related to Supabase Auth, permission rules, and shared inventory access.

| Edge Function
| Backend Function
| Implementation
| A server-side function used to execute backend logic outside the client application, especially for operations that require validation, scheduled execution, or cross-table coordination.
| In this project, Edge Functions are mainly discussed in the context of Supabase backend logic.

| Automation System
| System Component
| Requirements
| The system component responsible for evaluating inventory-related conditions such as low-stock, out-of-stock, near-expiry, and expired states.
| It detects relevant state transitions but does not directly own notification delivery.

| Notification Service
| System Component
| Requirements
| The system component responsible for creating and storing notification records after relevant state transitions are detected.
| Receives triggers from the Automation System; distinct from alert condition evaluation.

| Validation activity
| Process
| Requirements
| An activity used to check whether the system is addressing the right user needs, such as stakeholder reviews, scenario walkthroughs, usability checks, or feedback sessions.
| Related to Section 3.2 validation and verification.

| Acceptance criteria
| Requirement Detail
| Requirements
| A set of specific conditions that must be satisfied for a user story or requirement to be considered complete.
| Used to make user stories measurable and testable.

| End-to-end (E2E) test
| Verification Activity
| Requirements
| A test that checks a complete user workflow across multiple parts of the system, from user interaction through backend behavior and final result.
| Often used with acceptance testing to validate user-visible behavior.

| Deployment target
| Environment
| Implementation
| The platform, service, or environment where the system is intended to run for review, testing, staging, or release.
| Related to machine requirements, build procedure, deployment documentation, and external service configuration.

| Delete confirmation dialog
| Widget
| Implementation
| A modal dialog that requires explicit user confirmation before performing destructive actions.
| Implemented in `delete_confirmation_dialog.dart`; prevents accidental deletions.

| Error handler
| Utility
| Implementation
| A reusable utility that converts technical exceptions into user-friendly error messages with titles, actions, and error types.
| Implemented in `error_handler.dart`; used by InventoryCubit and UI components.

| Error state widget
| Widget
| Implementation
| A UI component that displays user-friendly error messages with a retry button for recoverable failures.
| Implemented in `error_state_widget.dart`; used on loading failure screens.

| Validation rule
| Rule
| Implementation
| An explicit constraint on user input that prevents invalid data from being submitted to the system.
| Implemented in `form_validators.dart`; includes name length, quantity range, and date format rules.
|===
